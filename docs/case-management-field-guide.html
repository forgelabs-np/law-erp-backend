Part 1 · How it works (plain language)

Think of the system as a three-level stack. Every piece of a dispute is stored at exactly the level where it belongs, so nothing is ever duplicated and every screen can be traced back to the same source data.

A DISPUTE IS MODELED AS 3 LAYERS ┌─────────────────────────────┐ MATTER (the story) │ "Ram vs Shyam — boundary │ one record, forever │ dispute, plot no. 45" │ e.g. TLAW-MAT-2026-00001 └──────────────┬──────────────┘ │ owns a chain of ┌──────────────▼──────────────┐ COURT CASE (one court) │ DC1 District Court │ one row PER COURT the case │ HC1 High Court (appeal) │ has been registered in │ SC1 Supreme Court (review) │ linked by parentCourtCaseId └──────────────┬──────────────┘ │ owns a diary of ┌──────────────▼──────────────┐ COURT EVENT (one date) │ #1 TARIK 2026-01-10 │ every diary date (Tarik) │ #2 PESHI 2026-02-05 │ and hearing (Peshi), │ #3 TARIK 2026-03-02 │ chained by nextEventId └─────────────────────────────┘

1.1 Matter — the dispute

A Matter is the whole dispute as the firm tracks it: who is suing whom, what type of case it is (civil or criminal), which court it started in, and which partner owns it. It is created once and never re-created — everything that happens later attaches to it.

1.2 CourtCase — one row per court

When the dispute is filed in a court — first at District, then later on appeal at High Court, then possibly at Supreme Court — each registration is a separate CourtCase. They are linked in a chain (parentCourtCaseId), so the whole journey is visible: DC1 → HC1 → SC1, and even remands back down to DC2. Each CourtCase has its own stage, status, judge, advocate, and its own diary of events.

1.3 CourtEvent — the diary (Tarik & Peshi)

Every date the court gives is a CourtEvent. Nepali court practice has two kinds:

  • TARIK (तारेख) — an administrative date: a formality, often handled by a junior or clerk. “Come back on this date.”
  • PESHI (पेशी) — a real hearing: arguments, evidence, orders, judgment.

Events are numbered per court case (#1, #2, #3 …) and chained: when today's event is marked “held”, the system asks “what did the court give next?” and creates the next event automatically — exactly like writing the next date in a lawyer's diary, but structured and impossible to lose.

1.4 Parties & roles

  • MatterParty = who the person is (name, phone, email, address). Created once per dispute.
  • CourtCaseRole = what role they play in this particular court (plaintiff/defendant/appellant…). Roles flip on appeal — the defendant who lost at District becomes the appellant at High Court.
  • Representation = REPRESENTED (we act for them), OPPOSING (we act against them), SELF (self-represented).

1.5 Timeline & audit

Every meaningful action (matter created, event scheduled, event held, judgment recorded, appeal filed) writes an immutable timeline entry. The firm can show “everything that ever happened” per matter, or firm-wide as one feed. A separate audit log records who did what, when.

1.6 Judgment & appeal deadlines

When a judgment is recorded, the system immediately computes the statutory appeal window (e.g. 30 days for a civil appeal from District Court) from a seeded rule table and stores the appeal deadline on the case. A nightly job watches those deadlines; a watch-list endpoint tells the firm which cases' windows are closing soon, and cases where the window lapsed with no appeal are marked final and the matter is nudged to DORMANT. Missing an appeal window is often irreversible — this is a first-class feature on purpose.

Part 3 · How a case flows (the machinery)

3.1 The Tarik/Peshi diary loop

This is the heart of the system — the digital equivalent of writing the next date in the diary.

schedule on the day, mark HELD: ┌──────────────┐ POST /events ┌──────────────┐ POST /events/{id}/held │ #n TARIK │ ─────────────────▶ │ #n TARIK │ ──────────────────────────┐ │ SCHEDULED │ │ SCHEDULED │ │ └──────────────┘ └──────┬───────┘ │ ▲ │ "what did the court give next?" │ │ ▼ ▼ │ ┌──────────────────────────────┐ ┌────────────────────────────┐ │ │ nextEventType = TARIK/PESHI │ │ nextEventType = JUDGMENT │ └─────────────────────│ → creates event #n+1 │ │ → judgment recorded inline│ │ (loop continues) │ │ (needs judgmentDate + │ └──────────────────────────────┘ │ judgmentSummary) │ └────────────────────────────┘
  • Schedule: POST /court-cases/{ref}/events with the type, date, time, advocate. A firm-wide advocate-overlap check runs: Peshi conflicts are blocked (400); Tarik conflicts are allowed with a conflictWarning on the response (they are often handled by a clerk).
  • Mark held: POST /court-events/{id}/held requires the answer to “what did the court give next?”: TARIK/PESHI → the server creates the next event and links it via nextEventId; JUDGMENT → no event row (judgment gets recorded when delivered — inline in this same call); NONE → the case is closed at this event.
  • Judgment inline: if the outcome was JUDGMENT_DELIVERED, the call must also carry judgmentDate and judgmentSummary — a case can only become DECIDED with the judgment on record (this is enforced so the two judgment paths can never drift apart).

3.2 Stage machine

Each court case has a stage chosen from a state machine that knows which stages are legal for the combination of court level × matter type × relation type. The server validates every transition; the UI never needs to duplicate the machine — it asks GET /court-cases/{ref}/allowed-stages for the legal next moves.

CIVIL (District trial) CRIMINAL (District trial) FILED ─▶ SUMMONS_ISSUED FIR_REGISTERED ─▶ UNDER_INVESTIGATION ─▶ RESPONSE_PENDING ─▶ CHARGE_SHEET_FILED ─▶ MEDIATION ─▶ HEARING_STAGE ─▶ HEARING_STAGE └─ settlement → CLOSED HEARING_STAGE ─▶ JUDGMENT_AWAITED ─▶ JUDGMENT_DELIVERED ─▶ (APPEALED / FURTHER_APPEALED / REMANDED / EXECUTION / SENTENCING / CLOSED) APPEAL (High / Supreme / Specialized) WRIT (direct HC/SC filing) APPEAL_FILED ─▶ ADMITTED ─▶ HEARING_STAGE WRIT_FILED ─▶ ADMITTED ─▶ HEARING_STAGE └─▶ NOTICE_ISSUED ─▶ HEARING_STAGE ─▶ ORDER_ISSUED ─▶ CLOSED ... ─▶ JUDGMENT_AWAITED ─▶ JUDGMENT_DELIVERED ─▶ ... CLOSED is the only terminal stage.
Stages vs statuses. stage is the procedural position (fine-grained); status is the coarse state used by dashboards. They move together: entering JUDGMENT_AWAITED sets status JUDGMENT_AWAITED, entering JUDGMENT_DELIVERED sets DECIDED, entering EXECUTION sets ACTIVE (and clears appealLapsed), entering CLOSED sets CLOSED.

3.3 Judgment & the appeal-deadline engine

Recording a judgment (PUT /court-cases/{ref}/judgment, or inline on a held event) does five things in one transaction:

  1. Sets judgmentDate, judgmentSummary, decisionInFavorOfPartyId;
  2. Moves stage → JUDGMENT_DELIVERED and status → DECIDED;
  3. Computes appealDeadline from the seeded statutory rules;
  4. Sets appealRequiresLeave=true for High Court judgments (SC appeal = leave petition);
  5. Writes a JUDGMENT_RECORDED timeline entry + audit log.
Appealed fromTypeState partyWindowExtensionLeave needed?
DistrictCivil—30 days+15No
DistrictCriminalYes70 days+30No
DistrictCriminalNo30 days+30No
HighCivil—35 days+15Yes
HighCriminal—35 days+30Yes
HighCriminalYes70 days+30Yes

The watch list (GET /court-cases/appeal-deadlines?withinDays=30) lists every decided, not-lapsed case whose window closes within N days and no appeal has been filed yet. A nightly job (03:00) marks lapsed cases appealLapsed=true and nudges the matter to DORMANT. GET /matters/stale is the companion flag: matters whose current leaf hasn't had a real Peshi in N days.

3.4 The appeal chain (going to another court)

To appeal: POST /matters/{matterNumber}/court-cases with relationType=APPEAL, the new level, court name, and the roles on the new instance. The system:

  • Creates the child case (ref HC1), initial stage APPEAL_FILED;
  • Marks the parent APPEALED (or REMANDED for a remand) — except REVIEW, which leaves the parent untouched;
  • Moves the matter's currentCourtCaseId leaf to the child and sets the matter back to ACTIVE;
  • Rejects attaching a new proceeding to a closed parent (again, REVIEW is the one exception — it is filed after the judgment is final).

Example A — a normal case that runs its course (civil, District Court, no appeal)

The business story

On 1 Aug 2026 Ram Sharma sues Shyam Thapa at Kathmandu District Court over a boundary dispute (plot no. 45). The court issues summons; Shyam files a written response; mediation fails; two hearings happen; arguments close; judgment is delivered on 28 Oct in Ram's favour. The 30-day appeal window passes with no appeal, so the matter settles into DORMANT. One matter, one court case, six diary events.

Example B — a case that goes to another court on appeal (District → High Court)

The business story

Building on Example A: Shyam loses at District Court and instructs the firm to appeal to the High Court, Patan, within the 30-day window (filed 10 Nov). At High Court the roles flip — Shyam is now the appellant, Ram the respondent. The appeal is admitted, notices issue, hearings run, and on 15 Mar 2027 the High Court delivers judgment. Because it is a High Court judgment, a further appeal to the Supreme Court is a leave petition process: the deadline is 35 days (19 Apr 2027) and appealRequiresLeave=true.

Example C — a long-pending case: tarik → peshi → tarik → peshi → tarik → peshi

The business story

A criminal matter (theft charge) at District Court keeps getting pushed: the court gives a Tarik, the Tarik rolls into a Peshi, the Peshi gets adjourned, another Tarik, another Peshi… months go by with no real progress. This is exactly the pattern the system is built to make visible: the chained events show the diary, and the stale-matters endpoint flags it when no real Peshi has happened for 90 days.
